Medicare Coverage for Wheelchairs and Power Scooters: Exactly What You Qualify For

SM
By Dr. Sandra Mills, RN
🕐 8 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Medicare cover power wheelchairs?

Yes — Medicare Part B covers power wheelchairs (power-operated vehicles) when: you have a mobility limitation from a medical condition, you cannot use a cane or walker, you cannot self-propel a manual wheelchair, and you need the wheelchair to perform activities of daily living in your home. Coverage requires a face-to-face examination by your doctor, a detailed written order, and the equipment from a Medicare-enrolled DME supplier.

Does Medicare cover motorized scooters?

Medicare covers power-operated vehicles (scooters) under the same rules as power wheelchairs — when medically necessary for in-home mobility. However, Medicare's coverage focus is on the home — a scooter prescribed primarily for outdoor or community use may not qualify. The mobility limitation must affect your ability to perform activities of daily living within your home.

What does Medicare NOT cover for wheelchairs?

Medicare does not cover: wheelchairs primarily for outdoor or recreational use, power wheelchairs when you can safely use a manual wheelchair, upgrades to premium models beyond the basic medically necessary configuration, and any wheelchair obtained without a proper physician face-to-face evaluation and documentation. Medicare also doesn't cover power wheelchairs for convenience rather than medical necessity.

How do I get a wheelchair covered by Medicare?

Step 1: Your doctor conducts a face-to-face examination documenting your mobility limitation and medical necessity. Step 2: Your doctor writes a detailed order (Certificate of Medical Necessity). Step 3: You obtain the wheelchair from a Medicare-enrolled DME supplier — always verify Medicare enrollment before ordering. Step 4: The supplier bills Medicare Part B — you pay 20% coinsurance.

Are Medicare power wheelchair scams common?

Yes — Medicare wheelchair fraud is one of the most common Medicare scams. Warning signs: anyone contacts you unsolicited offering a free power wheelchair in exchange for your Medicare number, a company claims Medicare will cover 100% with no copay for a high-end chair, or you're offered a wheelchair without a doctor's evaluation. Report suspicious offers to 1-800-MEDICARE or the HHS OIG fraud hotline at 1-800-HHS-TIPS.

Medicare covers mobility equipment including manual wheelchairs, power wheelchairs, and scooters — but the documentation requirements are strict, and the program is heavily targeted by fraud. Understanding the legitimate qualification process protects you from both coverage denials and fraudulent offers.

What Medicare Covers

Manual Wheelchairs

Standard manual wheelchairs are covered when prescribed by a physician for a qualifying medical condition. Coverage includes the basic wheelchair and necessary accessories (leg rests, arm rests, anti-tip devices). You pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after the Part B deductible. Lightweight and ultra-lightweight manual wheelchairs are covered when the patient's condition specifically requires the lighter weight — with documentation.

Power Wheelchairs (Power-Operated Vehicles)

Power wheelchairs receive the most scrutiny because they are the most expensive and most targeted by fraud. Medicare covers power wheelchairs when all four conditions are met: a severe mobility limitation due to a medical condition, inability to walk with a cane or walker, inability to safely self-propel a manual wheelchair, and a need for the chair to perform activities of daily living in the home.

Power Scooters

Covered under the same criteria as power wheelchairs. Medicare evaluates whether a scooter vs. a power wheelchair is appropriate based on the patient's ability to operate the device and their specific mobility needs. A patient who can operate a scooter but not a power wheelchair may be directed to the less expensive option.

The Strict Qualification Process

Medicare has tightened wheelchair documentation requirements significantly after widespread fraud. The current process requires:

  1. Face-to-face physician examination — not a telephone or telehealth visit for this purpose. Your doctor physically evaluates your mobility limitations.
  2. Detailed written order — the Certificate of Medical Necessity must document the specific mobility limitation, functional assessment, and why less expensive options (cane, walker, manual wheelchair) are insufficient.
  3. Medicare-enrolled DME supplier — use Medicare's supplier directory at medicare.gov to verify enrollment. Using a non-enrolled supplier means full cost comes out of pocket.
  4. Supplier documentation — the supplier must also document that the equipment was delivered and is appropriate for the patient's home environment.

The Home Mobility Rule

Medicare's coverage focus is on indoor home mobility — the ability to perform activities of daily living within your home. This distinction matters significantly. If your mobility limitation primarily affects outdoor or community activities but you can navigate your home adequately without a power wheelchair, Medicare coverage may be denied. The documentation must specifically address how the wheelchair is needed for in-home function.

Protecting Yourself From Wheelchair Fraud

Power wheelchair fraud costs Medicare hundreds of millions of dollars annually and targets seniors directly. Never accept a power wheelchair offer from anyone who contacts you unsolicited. Any legitimate Medicare wheelchair coverage begins with your doctor — not with a company calling to offer you a free chair. If a company asks for your Medicare number to check eligibility and says you qualify for a chair you didn't ask about — hang up.
