Medicare Advantage vs Medigap: Which Is Right for You in 2027?

SM
By Dr. Sandra Mills, RN
🕐 10 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between Medicare Advantage and Medigap?

Medicare Advantage (Part C) replaces Original Medicare through a private insurance plan — it bundles Parts A, B, and usually D with a network of providers and potential extra benefits. Medigap (Medicare Supplement) works alongside Original Medicare Parts A and B, covering the cost-sharing gaps that Original Medicare leaves — you keep your freedom to see any Medicare-accepting doctor in the country. The fundamental trade-off: Medicare Advantage offers lower premiums and extra benefits but restricts you to a network. Medigap offers complete freedom with predictable costs but no extra benefits.

Is Medicare Advantage or Medigap more popular?

Medicare Advantage enrollment has surpassed Original Medicare — approximately 54% of Medicare beneficiaries are now in Medicare Advantage plans. However, popularity doesn't determine which is better for a specific individual. Seniors with complex medical conditions, multiple specialists, or who value provider freedom consistently report higher satisfaction with Original Medicare + Medigap. Healthier seniors who rarely need specialist care often do well in Medicare Advantage at lower cost.

Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to Medigap?

You can switch during AEP (October 15–December 7) or during the January 1–March 31 Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period. However, switching to Medigap after your initial enrollment period means you'll likely face medical underwriting — insurers can deny coverage or charge higher premiums based on your health history in most states. This is the most important reason to make the right choice when you first turn 65.

What does Medicare Advantage cost vs Medigap?

Medicare Advantage: often $0–50/month plan premium on top of the Part B premium you pay either way, with copays and coinsurance for every service, and out-of-pocket maximums of $3,000–$8,000/year. Original Medicare + Medigap Plan G: $100–200/month Medigap premium plus $202.90 Part B premium plus Part D premium — typically $350–450/month total, but $0 after the $283 annual deductible for all covered services. At modest healthcare use, Advantage costs less. At significant healthcare use, Medigap typically costs less overall.

Does Medicare Advantage have prior authorization?

Yes — Medicare Advantage plans require prior authorization for many procedures, specialist visits, and services. This is the most common complaint about Medicare Advantage. Plans denied 17% of prior authorization requests in federal audits, though appeals succeed in 40–50% of cases. Original Medicare with Medigap has no prior authorization requirements — you and your doctor make care decisions without insurance company approval.

This is the most consequential Medicare decision most seniors make — and it's often made with incomplete information during a confusing enrollment period. Neither option is universally better: the right choice depends on your health status, financial situation, preferred providers, and how you value freedom versus cost.

The Core Difference

Think of it this way:

  • Original Medicare + Medigap: Maximum freedom, maximum predictability. See any Medicare doctor anywhere in the country. Pay your monthly premiums and after a small annual deductible, pay nothing else for covered care — no matter how much you use.
  • Medicare Advantage: Lower upfront cost, possible extra benefits (dental, vision, fitness), but restricted to a network, subject to prior authorization, and potentially significant cost-sharing if you need substantial care.

When Medicare Advantage Makes More Sense

  • You are generally healthy and rarely need specialist care or procedures
  • You live in an area with high-quality, highly-rated Advantage plans (4–5 star)
  • Your doctors are in the plan's network and you're comfortable staying in network
  • Extra benefits (dental, vision, hearing, gym) are services you'll actually use
  • Budget is a significant constraint and the lower monthly premium matters
  • You're comfortable navigating prior authorization processes when needed

When Original Medicare + Medigap Makes More Sense

  • You have complex health conditions and see multiple specialists regularly
  • You travel frequently or divide time between states (Advantage plans have service areas)
  • You want specific hospitals or cancer centers available to you without network restrictions
  • You want predictable costs — the monthly premium is your maximum, no surprise bills
  • Prior authorization delays would be particularly burdensome given your health situation
  • You're newly Medicare-eligible — locking in Medigap during your OEP guarantees acceptance at standard rates

The Prior Authorization Reality

This deserves emphasis because it's the most common source of Medicare Advantage dissatisfaction. When your doctor recommends a procedure, specialist referral, or medication, a Medicare Advantage plan may require prior authorization — the plan's medical team must approve the service before you receive it. Delays of days to weeks are common. Denials occur in 17% of cases, though appeals succeed in 40–50% of cases with physician documentation.

With Original Medicare: no prior authorization for covered services. Your doctor recommends it, you receive it. The insurance company's involvement is limited to billing.

The Switching Risk

If you choose Medicare Advantage at 65 and later want to switch to Medigap (perhaps after developing a chronic condition), you face medical underwriting in most states. An insurer can deny your Medigap application or charge significantly more based on your current health. This asymmetry — easy to switch from Medigap to Advantage, potentially difficult to switch the other way — is a strong argument for choosing Medigap at initial enrollment if there's uncertainty about which is right long-term.

  • If you're newly 65 and uncertain: Medigap provides more flexibility long-term
  • If you're budget-constrained and healthy: Advantage may make financial sense
  • If you have complex health needs or see multiple specialists: Medigap typically wins
  • Always verify your doctors are in-network before choosing Advantage
  • Check star ratings — only consider Advantage plans rated 4 stars or higher
