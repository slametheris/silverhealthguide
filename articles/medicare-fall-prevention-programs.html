Medicare-Covered Fall Prevention Programs: What's Available and How to Access Them

JW
By Dr. James Whitfield, PT, DPT
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Medicare cover fall prevention classes?

Medicare Part B covers fall prevention interventions as part of the Annual Wellness Visit and through specific community-based programs. Structured fall prevention programs like Stepping On, A Matter of Balance, and Tai Chi for Arthritis are recognized by the CDC and in many areas are offered through Medicare Advantage plans, senior centers, and YMCAs at reduced or no cost. Physical therapy for balance and fall risk is covered under Part B when prescribed by a physician.

What is the Stepping On fall prevention program?

Stepping On is a 7-week, 2-hour community workshop for adults at risk of falling. Evidence: participants have 31% fewer falls than control groups. It covers exercise, medication review, vision and home safety, and managing fear of falling. Offered by trained community facilitators at YMCAs, senior centers, and churches. Many Medicare Advantage plans cover or subsidize participation. Find a local program at stepping-on.org.

Does Medicare cover physical therapy for fall prevention?

Yes — Medicare Part B covers physical therapy when prescribed by a physician as medically necessary. For fall prevention, a PT performs a comprehensive balance and gait assessment and creates a personalized exercise program. There is no annual cap on medically necessary PT. You pay 20% coinsurance after the Part B deductible ($0 with Medigap Plan G). Ask your doctor for a PT referral if you have had a fall or are afraid of falling.

Is Tai Chi effective for fall prevention in seniors?

Yes — Tai Chi is among the best-studied fall prevention interventions in seniors. A systematic review of 10,000+ participants showed Tai Chi reduces fall risk by 20–45%. It improves balance, proprioception, leg strength, and reaction time simultaneously. Tai Chi for Arthritis and Tai Chi for Balance are specific programs endorsed by the National Council on Aging. Many YMCAs and senior centers offer these programs, often subsidized for Medicare beneficiaries.

What is the STEADI program for fall prevention?

STEADI (Stopping Elderly Accidents, Deaths, and Injuries) is a CDC initiative for healthcare providers to screen and address fall risk. Your doctor can screen you using the STEADI screening tools at your Annual Wellness Visit — this is already built into the AWV protocol. The screen identifies fall risk factors and leads to targeted interventions: medication review, vision correction, PT referral, and home safety assessment.

Falls are the leading cause of injury death in adults over 65 — and Medicare covers far more fall prevention intervention than most seniors know. From evidence-based community programs to covered physical therapy to home safety assessments, multiple pathways exist. Accessing them requires knowing they exist and asking for them specifically.

Medicare-Covered Clinical Interventions

Physical Therapy — Part B Covered

A physical therapist with fall prevention expertise performs a comprehensive assessment: Timed Up and Go test, Berg Balance Scale, gait analysis, and muscle strength evaluation. They then create a targeted home exercise program addressing your specific deficits — the most important of which are usually hip abductor strength, ankle dorsiflexion, and proprioception. Medicare Part B covers PT with no annual cap when medically necessary. Ask your doctor: "Can you write me a referral for physical therapy focused on fall prevention and balance?"

STEADI Fall Risk Screening at Annual Wellness Visit

The CDC's STEADI protocol is incorporated into Medicare's Annual Wellness Visit. Your doctor should ask about falls in the past year, test your walking speed and balance (Timed Up and Go), and review your medications for fall risk. If you've had a fall or feel unsteady, bring it up proactively — don't wait for the provider to ask. This triggers a cascade of appropriate interventions including medication review, vision referral, and PT.

Medication Review

Medications cause or contribute to approximately 40% of falls in seniors. Blood pressure medications (dizziness), sleep aids (sedation, balance impairment), antihistamines (confusion), and certain antidepressants all increase fall risk. A pharmacist medication review can identify high-risk medications. Ask your doctor or pharmacist: "Are any of my medications increasing my fall risk?"

Evidence-Based Community Programs

Stepping On

A 7-week, 2-hour weekly workshop (plus one home visit) with a trained facilitator. Covers fall prevention exercises, medication and vision safety, footwear, and home hazard reduction. Evidence: 31% reduction in falls. Available at YMCAs, senior centers, hospitals, and churches. Many Medicare Advantage plans subsidize or cover it. Find a program at stepping-on.org.

A Matter of Balance

An 8-session, 2-hour program focused on reducing fear of falling — which itself causes falls through restriction of activity and deconditioning. Evidence: participants have significantly fewer fall-related injuries at 12 months. Available through Area Agencies on Aging, senior centers, and hospitals. Find a program at mainehealth.org/maob or through your local AAA.

Tai Chi for Balance and Arthritis

12-week programs (Tai Chi for Arthritis, Tai Chi for Fall Prevention) taught by certified instructors. Most effective sustained fall prevention intervention in literature — 20–45% fall reduction, benefits continuing for 2+ years. YMCAs, senior centers, and community centers in most areas offer these programs. Search "Tai Chi for Better Balance" + your city for local classes.

YMCA/SilverSneakers Classes

SilverSneakers (included in many Medicare Advantage plans) and YMCA Active Older Adults programs include balance-specific classes. Free with plan membership. Classes specifically designed for older adults with instructors trained in senior fitness.
