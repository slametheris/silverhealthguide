Medicare Part D in 2027: $2,400 Cap, $700 Deductible, and What It Means for You

MC
By Margaret Chen, PharmD
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Medicare Part D's protection against runaway drug costs continues in 2027 — at a higher threshold. The annual out-of-pocket cap rises from $2,100 to $2,400, and the highest deductible a plan can charge rises from $615 to $700. Here is what that means for your budget and your plan choice.

The 2027 Numbers

  • Out-of-pocket cap: $2,400 (up from $2,100 in 2026; $2,000 when it began in 2025)
  • Maximum deductible: $700 (up from $615)
  • National base beneficiary premium: $41.33 (up from $38.99) — a benchmark, not what you pay

Once your spending on covered drugs reaches the cap, you pay $0 for covered Part D drugs for the rest of the calendar year. Your monthly premium does not count toward the cap, and neither does spending on drugs that aren't on your plan's formulary.

Who Feels the Change Most

If you take mostly inexpensive generics, you probably never get near the cap, so the bigger factor for you is your plan's premium, deductible, and copays. If you take costly brand-name or specialty drugs, you will likely hit the cap early in the year; the $300 increase means a larger total before costs stop.

Premiums May Move

A federal program that helped hold down standalone Part D premiums during 2025 and 2026 ends after 2026. Analysts differ on how much premiums will change, and individual plans vary widely. The only reliable answer is your plan's own 2027 premium, which is in your Annual Notice of Change and on Medicare.gov.

Spreading Costs With the Payment Plan

The Medicare Prescription Payment Plan lets you pay your drug costs in monthly installments instead of at the pharmacy counter. It doesn't lower what you owe, but it can smooth out big bills early in the year. See our guide to the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan.

How to Check Your Plan

  1. Open your Annual Notice of Change and note the 2027 premium and deductible
  2. Check each medication's tier and restrictions on the 2027 drug list
  3. Run Medicare.gov Plan Finder with all your drugs and your pharmacy
  4. Compare total estimated annual cost, not just premium
  5. Enroll by December 7 if you decide to switch
  • Know the cap is $2,400 and the maximum deductible is $700 in 2027
  • Check your drugs are on the formulary — non-formulary spending doesn't count toward the cap
  • Compare total annual cost across plans before December 7
  • Remember the $35 monthly insulin cap continues for covered insulins

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Part D out-of-pocket cap in 2027?

$2,400. It was $2,100 in 2026 and $2,000 when the cap began in 2025.

What is the maximum Part D deductible in 2027?

$700, up from $615 in 2026. Many plans charge less than the maximum.

Do premiums count toward the cap?

No. Only your out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs counts toward the cap.

Does the $35 insulin cap continue?

Yes. Covered insulin is capped at $35 per month per product under Part D.
