Flu, COVID, and RSV Vaccines for Seniors in 2026: Which Ones, When, and Why

SM
By Dr. Sandra Mills, RN
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Frequently Asked Questions

Which flu vaccine should seniors get in 2026?

Seniors 65+ should get a high-dose or adjuvanted flu vaccine — not the standard flu shot. Three options are specifically approved for seniors: Fluzone High-Dose Quadrivalent (4x the antigen of standard), FLUAD Quadrivalent (adjuvanted with MF59), and Flublok Quadrivalent (recombinant, egg-free). Studies consistently show these produce stronger immune responses and better protection than standard flu shots in older adults. All are covered at $0 under Medicare Part D.

Does Medicare cover flu shots?

Yes — the flu vaccine is covered at $0 under Medicare Part D every fall. You do not need a doctor visit or prescription. Walk into any Medicare-participating pharmacy (CVS, Walgreens, Walmart, most grocery store pharmacies) and show your Medicare card. Request specifically a high-dose or adjuvanted vaccine — pharmacists have them and may not offer them by default without you asking.

Should seniors get the updated COVID vaccine in fall 2026?

Yes — CDC recommends that adults 65+ receive the updated annual COVID vaccine in fall, ideally September–October before respiratory virus season peaks. Seniors are at significantly higher risk for severe COVID complications including hospitalization and long COVID. The updated vaccine is $0 under Medicare Part D. Getting it alongside (or close to) the flu shot is convenient and safe.

What is the RSV vaccine for seniors?

RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus) causes severe respiratory illness in adults over 60 — more hospitalizations than previously recognized. The FDA has approved RSV vaccines for adults 60+: Abrysvo (Pfizer), Arexvy (GSK), and mResvia (Moderna). CDC recommends a single lifetime dose for adults 60+ using shared clinical decision-making with their doctor. RSV vaccine is covered at $0 under Medicare Part D. If you haven't received it, ask your doctor or pharmacist this fall.

Can seniors get multiple vaccines at the same visit?

Yes — flu, COVID, and RSV vaccines can all be given at the same visit with no safety concerns. You may experience more soreness at injection sites when getting multiple vaccines simultaneously, but this is temporary and not harmful. Getting all three at once is convenient and ensures you don't forget one. Most pharmacies can administer all three in a single visit.

Fall 2026 is respiratory virus season — and seniors face significantly higher risks from influenza, COVID-19, and RSV than younger adults. The good news: all recommended vaccines are $0 under Medicare Part D, and they can all be obtained at your local pharmacy without a doctor visit. Here is exactly what seniors need and when.

🍂 Fall Vaccine Timing

Ideal window: September–October 2026. Getting vaccinated before November ensures peak protection through the December–February peak of flu and COVID season. Protection from flu vaccine builds over 2 weeks.

Flu Vaccine — Seniors Need a Different Formula

This is the detail most seniors miss: standard flu shots provide significantly weaker protection in adults over 65. The aging immune system responds less robustly to standard-dose vaccines. Three formulations specifically designed and tested for seniors provide meaningfully better protection:

  • Fluzone High-Dose Quadrivalent: Contains 4x the antigen of standard flu shots. Studies show 24% more effective at preventing flu in seniors than standard dose.
  • FLUAD Quadrivalent: Contains MF59 adjuvant that amplifies the immune response. Strong evidence for better protection in seniors 65+.
  • Flublok Quadrivalent: Recombinant (egg-free) — appropriate for those with egg allergies. Also higher antigen content than standard shots.

When you go to the pharmacy, specifically request: "I'd like the high-dose flu vaccine for seniors." All three are covered at $0 under Medicare Part D.

COVID Vaccine — Updated Annual Shot

Like the flu vaccine, the COVID vaccine is updated annually to match circulating variants. The fall 2026 updated COVID vaccine is recommended for all adults, with particular urgency for seniors 65+ and those with chronic conditions. Seniors are at higher risk for COVID hospitalization, ICU admission, and long COVID complications. The updated vaccine is $0 under Medicare Part D. Many pharmacies offer flu and COVID together in one visit — convenient and safe.

RSV Vaccine — One-Time, Life-Changing for Seniors

RSV hospitalizes approximately 177,000 older adults annually in the US — more than most people realize. FDA-approved RSV vaccines for adults 60+ (Abrysvo, Arexvy, mResvia) are recommended as a single lifetime dose. If you haven't received an RSV vaccine, ask your doctor or pharmacist this fall. All three RSV vaccines are covered at $0 under Medicare Part D.

Pneumococcal Vaccine — Don't Overlook This One

Pneumococcal pneumonia is a leading complication of both flu and COVID in seniors. If you haven't received Prevnar 20 (PCV20), discuss it with your doctor at your next visit. A single dose of PCV20 is now recommended for most adults who haven't previously been vaccinated against pneumococcal disease. Covered at $0 under Medicare Part B (not Part D — given in the doctor's office).

Shingrix — Two Doses That Most Seniors Haven't Completed

Shingrix (shingles vaccine) is a two-dose series, given 2–6 months apart, for adults 50+. It is 91% effective at preventing shingles — which can cause severe pain lasting months to years (postherpetic neuralgia). Both doses are $0 under Medicare Part D. If you've had chickenpox (almost everyone over 50 has), you're at risk for shingles. If you haven't started or completed Shingrix, this fall is the time.
