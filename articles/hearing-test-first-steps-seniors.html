Your First Hearing Test: What to Expect and When to See a Doctor First

SM
By Dr. Sandra Mills, RN
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Untreated hearing loss is linked to social withdrawal, falls, and faster cognitive decline — yet people wait years to be tested. A hearing test is quick, painless, and the first step toward deciding what, if anything, you need.

Signs It's Time to Get Tested

  • You ask people to repeat themselves, or often misunderstand words
  • You turn the TV up higher than others prefer
  • Conversations in restaurants or groups are hard to follow
  • You miss phone calls, doorbells, or alarms
  • Family members mention it

See a Doctor Promptly If You Have…

  • Sudden or rapidly worsening hearing loss — this can be an emergency; seek medical care within days
  • Hearing loss in only one ear, or much worse in one ear
  • Ear pain or drainage
  • Dizziness or balance problems
  • Ringing in only one ear

These can signal conditions that need medical evaluation before — or instead of — hearing aids.

What Happens at a Hearing Test

  1. History and ear check: the clinician asks about symptoms and looks in your ears for earwax or other problems.
  2. Pure-tone test: you wear headphones and press a button when you hear beeps at different pitches and volumes.
  3. Speech test: you repeat words at different volumes, sometimes with background noise.
  4. Results: an audiogram shows your hearing at each pitch, and the clinician explains whether the loss is mild, moderate, or more severe.

What Medicare Covers

Medicare Part B may cover a diagnostic hearing and balance exam when your doctor orders it to find out whether you need medical treatment. It does not cover routine hearing exams to fit hearing aids, and Original Medicare does not cover hearing aids themselves. Some Medicare Advantage plans include hearing exams and a hearing aid allowance — see our hearing aid coverage guide.

If Your Result Shows Mild to Moderate Loss

Adults with mild to moderate hearing loss can consider over-the-counter hearing aids, which don't require a prescription. A baseline hearing test first helps you know whether OTC aids are appropriate. Prescription aids fitted by an audiologist are an option if your loss is greater or more complex. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Medicare cover hearing tests?

Part B may cover a diagnostic hearing and balance exam when your doctor orders it to diagnose a medical problem. Routine exams to fit hearing aids are not covered by Original Medicare.

Do I need a hearing test before buying OTC hearing aids?

It isn't required, but a baseline test tells you the degree of your loss and whether OTC aids are appropriate.

How often should seniors get their hearing checked?

If you notice problems, get tested now. Many clinicians suggest periodic checks after age 60 or 65, especially if you have risk factors.
