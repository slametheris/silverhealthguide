Medicare Coverage When Traveling: What's Covered in the US and What Isn't Abroad

SM
By Dr. Sandra Mills, RN
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Medicare cover travel emergencies in the US?

Yes — Original Medicare covers emergency care anywhere in the United States. If you have a medical emergency while traveling domestically, any Medicare-participating hospital must provide emergency stabilization. You pay the same Part A deductible and Part B coinsurance as at home. Medicare Advantage plans are more complicated — HMO plans typically only cover emergencies outside the service area, not routine care.

Does Medicare cover medical care outside the US?

Generally no — Original Medicare does not cover medical care received outside the United States, US territories, or US-flagged ships within 6 hours of US port. There are very limited exceptions. This is a critical gap for seniors who travel internationally. Travel health insurance or specific international health coverage is strongly recommended for international travel.

Do Medigap plans cover international travel?

Some Medigap plans include international travel emergency coverage. Medigap Plans C, D, F, G, M, and N cover 80% of foreign travel emergency costs (after a $250 deductible) up to a $50,000 lifetime limit. If you have one of these plans and travel internationally, you have some protection — but the $50,000 lifetime limit can be exceeded by a single serious medical event.

Does Medicare Advantage cover international travel?

Most Medicare Advantage plans do not cover international care — they typically follow Original Medicare's approach of no international coverage. Some plans specifically designed for travelers or expatriates may include international coverage. Read your plan's Evidence of Coverage document carefully and call your plan before traveling internationally.

What should seniors do for medical coverage when traveling abroad?

Purchase travel health insurance before every international trip. Look specifically for: medical evacuation coverage (the most expensive risk — $50,000–$200,000 to medically evacuate from a remote location), at least $100,000 in emergency medical coverage, pre-existing condition coverage (specify that your conditions must be covered), 24/7 emergency assistance line, and direct payment to foreign hospitals rather than reimbursement.

Medicare was designed for care within the United States — and its international gaps catch seniors off guard at the worst possible time. A medical emergency abroad can cost $100,000+ before medical evacuation. Understanding what Medicare covers when you travel — and where it doesn't — is essential planning for any senior who travels.

US Travel — Comprehensive Coverage

Within the 50 United States, US territories (Puerto Rico, Guam, US Virgin Islands), and US-flagged ships, Original Medicare provides full coverage — the same as at home. Emergency rooms across the country must stabilize any Medicare patient. For Original Medicare beneficiaries, there is no network restriction for emergencies — you can go to any Medicare-participating hospital in the country.

Medicare Advantage and Domestic Travel

Medicare Advantage HMO plans have service areas and networks. Outside your plan's service area, HMO plans typically cover only emergencies and urgently needed care — not routine doctor visits or prescription refills. If you travel for extended periods, a Medicare Advantage PPO (which covers out-of-network care at higher cost) or returning to Original Medicare during AEP may be more appropriate.

International Travel — The Coverage Gap

Original Medicare covers almost nothing internationally. The specific exceptions are narrow: emergency care on a foreign ship within US territorial waters (within 6 hours of port), care at a Canadian hospital when a medical emergency occurs while traveling the most direct route between Alaska and another US state, and care at a Mexican hospital when the hospital is closer to the patient's US home than any US hospital and a medical emergency occurs.

Outside these specific scenarios, Original Medicare pays nothing internationally. You receive the bill directly from the foreign provider, and Medicare provides no reimbursement.

Medigap International Coverage

Medigap Plans C, D, F, G, M, and N include international travel emergency coverage. Coverage: 80% of foreign emergency medical costs after a $250 deductible, up to a $50,000 lifetime maximum. This provides meaningful but not unlimited protection. Note the $50,000 lifetime maximum — not annual, but lifetime across all international travel. A single serious international event can exhaust this limit permanently.

Travel Health Insurance — What to Buy

For international travel, purchase separate travel health insurance covering:

  • Emergency medical evacuation: $200,000+ — this is the most important coverage. Air ambulance from a remote location to a US hospital can cost $100,000–$500,000.
  • Emergency medical coverage: At least $100,000 for hospitalization and treatment
  • Pre-existing condition coverage: Must explicitly cover your conditions — not just "emergency" coverage that excludes your pre-existing conditions
  • 24/7 assistance line: Direct coordination with foreign hospitals and evacuation services
  • Direct billing to hospitals: Avoid needing to pay out of pocket and seek reimbursement

Reputable travel insurance providers include Allianz, Travel Guard, IMG Global, and GeoBlue. Annual multi-trip policies are cost-effective for seniors who travel internationally more than once per year.

  • Within the US: Original Medicare provides full coverage — no action needed
  • Medicare Advantage HMO: only emergencies covered outside your service area
  • International travel: purchase travel health insurance before every trip
  • Prioritize medical evacuation coverage — this is the catastrophic cost risk
  • Check your Medigap plan — Plans C, D, F, G, M, N include $50,000 lifetime international emergency coverage
  • Carry a copy of your Medicare card and all current prescriptions when traveling
