Medicare Coverage for Stroke Rehabilitation: Inpatient, Outpatient, and Home Care

RO
By Dr. Rachel Okonkwo, MD, Neurology
📅 July 27, 2026
🕐 9 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Medicare cover stroke rehabilitation?

Yes — Medicare covers comprehensive stroke rehabilitation through multiple settings: inpatient rehabilitation facilities (IRFs), skilled nursing facilities (SNFs), outpatient therapy, and home health services. The appropriate level of care depends on the severity of deficits and ability to participate in intensive therapy. Most stroke survivors access multiple settings during recovery.

What is an inpatient rehabilitation facility for stroke?

An inpatient rehabilitation facility (IRF) provides intensive multidisciplinary rehabilitation in a hospital setting — at least 3 hours of therapy daily, 5–7 days per week. IRFs are covered by Medicare Part A after a qualifying inpatient hospital stay. They provide the most intensive rehabilitation and are appropriate for stroke survivors who can tolerate and benefit from intensive therapy.

How long does Medicare cover stroke rehab?

Medicare covers inpatient stroke rehab (IRF or SNF) for as long as you meet medical necessity criteria and show functional progress. For skilled nursing facility care, Medicare covers up to 100 days per benefit period (days 1–20 free, days 21–100 with $209.50/day copay in 2026). For outpatient therapy, there is no session limit for medically necessary treatment.

Does Medicare cover speech therapy after stroke?

Yes — Medicare covers speech-language pathology therapy after stroke, including treatment for aphasia (language difficulties), dysarthria (speech clarity), and dysphagia (swallowing difficulties). Speech therapy is covered in all settings: inpatient rehab, skilled nursing, outpatient, and home health. Coverage continues as long as progress is documented.

What should families do immediately after a stroke?

The first priority is rapid medical treatment — call 911 immediately at the first signs of stroke (FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911). During hospitalization, ask the medical team about inpatient rehabilitation placement — rehabilitation hospitals generally achieve better outcomes than skilled nursing facilities for appropriate patients. Early rehab initiation within 24–72 hours of stroke improves outcomes.

Stroke is the leading cause of long-term disability in adults — and the quality and intensity of rehabilitation in the weeks and months following a stroke has a greater impact on recovery than any other factor. Medicare covers comprehensive rehabilitation, but families often don't know what to ask for or which settings provide the best recovery outcomes. Here is everything you need to know.

72 hours
Early rehabilitation should begin within 72 hours of stroke onset — the brain's neuroplasticity (ability to reorganize and form new connections) is highest immediately after stroke and in the following weeks

The 4 Rehabilitation Settings Medicare Covers

Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility (IRF) — Highest Intensity

IRFs provide 3+ hours of multidisciplinary therapy daily (physical, occupational, and speech therapy) in a hospital setting. Studies consistently show better functional outcomes from IRF compared to skilled nursing facility rehabilitation for appropriate candidates. Medicare Part A covers IRF after a qualifying hospital stay — you pay the Part A deductible and daily coinsurance. With Medigap Plan G, you pay $0.

Not everyone qualifies for IRF — patients must be medically stable and able to tolerate at least 3 hours of therapy daily. Ask your hospital team directly: "Is my family member a candidate for an inpatient rehabilitation hospital?"

Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF) — Intermediate

SNF rehabilitation provides therapy once or twice daily in a less intensive setting. After a qualifying 3-day inpatient hospital stay, Medicare covers SNF rehab — days 1–20 free, days 21–100 at $209.50/day coinsurance in 2026. Appropriate for stroke survivors who need rehabilitation but cannot tolerate the intensity of an IRF, or who need more nursing care alongside therapy.

Outpatient Therapy — Community-Based

Once discharge criteria are met, most stroke survivors continue physical, occupational, and speech therapy in outpatient settings. Medicare Part B covers outpatient therapy with no visit limit for medically necessary treatment. You pay 20% coinsurance after the Part B deductible. Outpatient therapy can continue for months or years as long as continued progress is documented.

Home Health Therapy — For Homebound Patients

For stroke survivors who cannot safely leave home for outpatient therapy, Medicare covers home physical, occupational, and speech therapy at $0 copay when homebound criteria are met. Home health is appropriate during the transition from inpatient care to outpatient and for those with permanent mobility limitations.

What Stroke Rehabilitation Covers

  • Physical therapy: Walking, balance, strength, transfers (bed to chair, etc.), fall prevention
  • Occupational therapy: Relearning activities of daily living — dressing, eating, bathing, hand function
  • Speech-language pathology: Language recovery (aphasia), speech clarity, swallowing safety (dysphagia)
  • Cognitive rehabilitation: Memory, attention, problem-solving, safety awareness
  • Psychological services: Post-stroke depression affects 30–40% of stroke survivors — Medicare covers evaluation and treatment
  • Recreational therapy: In IRF settings, helps regain meaningful activities

What Families Can Do to Improve Outcomes

Family involvement in stroke rehabilitation significantly improves outcomes. Attend therapy sessions when permitted — therapists can teach you how to continue exercises at home, how to assist safely with transfers, and communication strategies for aphasia. Do not enable avoidance of challenging activities — gentle encouragement to use the affected arm, leg, or speech is more beneficial than compensating for the person with stroke.

  • Advocate for inpatient rehabilitation hospital (IRF) placement if your family member can tolerate 3 hours/day of therapy
  • Begin asking about rehabilitation options during the acute hospitalization — do not wait for discharge planning
  • Attend therapy sessions when permitted to learn home exercise techniques
  • Post-stroke depression is treatable and common — watch for signs and discuss with the care team
  • Continue outpatient therapy for as long as progress occurs — Medicare has no visit limit for medically necessary care
  • With Medigap Plan G, inpatient rehabilitation coinsurance is covered — $0 out of pocket
