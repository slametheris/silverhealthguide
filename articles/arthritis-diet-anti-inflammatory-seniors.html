Frequently Asked Questions

What foods reduce arthritis inflammation?

The most anti-inflammatory foods for arthritis are fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel — omega-3), colorful vegetables and fruits (especially berries, cherries, leafy greens), olive oil, nuts (especially walnuts), turmeric with black pepper, ginger, and green tea. These foods reduce inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-6) that drive arthritis pain.

What foods make arthritis worse?

Foods that increase inflammation and worsen arthritis pain include refined sugar and high-fructose corn syrup, refined grains (white bread, white rice, pasta), trans fats and partially hydrogenated oils, processed meats (hot dogs, deli meats, sausage), excessive alcohol, and omega-6-heavy vegetable oils (corn oil, sunflower oil, soybean oil).

Does the Mediterranean diet help arthritis?

Yes — the Mediterranean diet consistently shows the strongest anti-inflammatory evidence of any dietary pattern. Multiple clinical trials show it significantly reduces CRP (a key inflammation marker), reduces arthritis pain and stiffness, and improves overall joint function. Key elements: olive oil as primary fat, fish twice weekly, abundant vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and limited red meat.

Does turmeric help arthritis?

Turmeric's active compound curcumin has anti-inflammatory properties comparable to some NSAIDs in clinical studies. The challenge: curcumin is poorly absorbed alone. Taking turmeric with black pepper (which contains piperine) increases absorption by up to 2,000%. Studies using highly bioavailable curcumin supplements show meaningful pain reduction in knee osteoarthritis.

Can diet reduce arthritis pain without medication?

Diet changes alone can significantly reduce arthritis pain and inflammation — particularly when combined with exercise and weight management. However, diet is most effective as part of a comprehensive approach, not a replacement for prescribed medications. Many people find that anti-inflammatory dietary changes reduce their medication needs — always discuss changes with your doctor.

What you eat directly affects joint inflammation — and for the estimated 60% of Americans over 65 with some form of arthritis, dietary changes can meaningfully reduce pain, stiffness, and disease progression. This is not alternative medicine: multiple clinical trials have demonstrated that specific dietary patterns reduce inflammatory markers by 20–30% in arthritis patients. Here is exactly what to eat and what to avoid.

Best Foods for Arthritis — Eat More of These

Fatty Fish (2–3 times per week)

Salmon, sardines, mackerel, herring, and trout are the most powerful anti-inflammatory foods available. Their EPA and DHA omega-3 fatty acids directly inhibit inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-1, IL-6) that drive arthritis pain. A 2006 study showed 2 servings of fatty fish per week reduced CRP by 26% in osteoarthritis patients. Canned sardines and tuna are the most affordable options.

Colorful Berries

Cherries (especially tart cherries), blueberries, strawberries, and raspberries contain anthocyanins — powerful antioxidants that specifically target uric acid (relevant for gout) and general joint inflammation. Studies show tart cherry juice reduces both gout attacks and osteoarthritis pain when consumed regularly.

Leafy Green Vegetables

Spinach, kale, Swiss chard, and broccoli contain vitamin K, folate, and antioxidants that reduce inflammatory markers. Vitamin K is particularly important for joint health — it is essential for proteoglycan synthesis in cartilage. Seniors on warfarin should discuss leafy green consumption with their doctor due to vitamin K interactions.

Extra Virgin Olive Oil

Extra virgin olive oil contains oleocanthal — a compound with anti-inflammatory effects similar to ibuprofen at equivalent doses. It also provides monounsaturated fats that reduce CRP. Use it as your primary cooking fat and for salad dressings. A 2-tablespoon daily serving provides meaningful anti-inflammatory benefit.

Turmeric + Black Pepper

Curcumin in turmeric has demonstrated anti-inflammatory effects comparable to some NSAIDs in clinical trials. The critical combination: always consume with black pepper, which increases curcumin absorption by up to 2,000%. Add turmeric and black pepper to soups, stews, and rice. For supplemental doses, look for curcumin with piperine (black pepper extract).

Foods to Avoid or Minimize

  • Added sugar: Directly triggers inflammatory cytokine release. Check labels — sugar hides in sauces, dressings, bread, and yogurt
  • Refined grains: White bread, white rice, pasta, and most packaged snacks spike blood sugar and promote inflammation
  • Processed meats: Hot dogs, deli meats, sausage, and bacon contain advanced glycation end products (AGEs) that increase joint inflammation
  • Vegetable oils high in omega-6: Corn oil, sunflower oil, soybean oil — excess omega-6 relative to omega-3 promotes inflammation. Use olive oil instead
  • Alcohol: Regular alcohol consumption increases uric acid (worsening gout) and promotes systemic inflammation
