Holiday Health and Safety for Seniors: Food, Medications, Falls, and Gatherings

SM
By Dr. Sandra Mills, RN
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

The holidays are a high point of the year for many seniors — and a higher-risk period for avoidable problems. A few simple habits around food, medications, falls, and gatherings can prevent most of them.

Food Safety

Older adults are more likely to become seriously ill from foodborne infections. Refrigerate leftovers within two hours, reheat them until steaming hot (165°F), and avoid foods left out all afternoon on a buffet. Cook turkey and other poultry to a safe internal temperature, and if you are at higher risk, avoid unpasteurized cheeses and juices and heat deli meats until steaming hot.

Medications and Travel

  • Pack all medications in your carry-on, in original bottles or a clearly labeled organizer
  • Bring at least two extra days' supply in case of delays
  • Carry a printed medication list and your doctor's phone number
  • Ask your pharmacist about refilling early if you will be away

Alcohol and Medications

Many common medicines — sleep aids, pain relievers, blood pressure drugs, diabetes medicines — interact with alcohol. If you drink, ask your pharmacist whether any of your medications warrant avoiding alcohol entirely, and keep intake low. Never combine alcohol with sedating medicines.

Falls: Ice, Ladders, and Clutter

  • Skip the ladder — ask someone younger to hang lights and decorations
  • Keep walkways clear of extension cords and holiday clutter
  • Wear rubber-soled shoes outdoors and take your time on ice
  • Use night lights between bedroom and bathroom, especially in unfamiliar guest rooms

Heating and Home Safety

Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, keep space heaters at least three feet from anything flammable, never use a stove or oven to heat the home, and keep real candles away from decorations.

Gatherings and Illness

Before indoor gatherings, make sure you have your flu, COVID, and RSV vaccines if recommended for you; see our fall vaccine guide. Ask guests who feel sick to stay home, and consider a smaller gathering if you have a chronic condition.

Loneliness and Grief

The holidays can intensify loneliness, especially after a loss. Plan something to look forward to, ask a friend to check in, or join a community or faith gathering. If sadness lingers beyond a few weeks or you feel hopeless, talk to your doctor; if you are in crisis, call or text 988.

  • Refrigerate leftovers within two hours and reheat to 165°F
  • Carry medications and a medication list when traveling
  • Check alcohol interactions with your pharmacist
  • Skip ladders and ice; keep walkways clear
  • Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms
  • Reach out if the season feels lonely

Frequently Asked Questions

What holiday foods are risky for older adults?

Foods left at room temperature for hours, undercooked poultry, unpasteurized dairy and juices, and cold deli meats that aren't heated are the most common risks. Older adults are more vulnerable to serious foodborne illness.

How long can leftovers sit out?

Refrigerate within two hours. If the room is hot, refrigerate sooner.

Can I drink alcohol with my medications?

Many medications interact with alcohol. Ask your pharmacist about your specific medicines, and avoid alcohol with sedatives or sleep aids.
