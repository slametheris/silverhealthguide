Veterans Benefits and Medicare: How They Work Together in 2026

RH
By Robert Hargrove, CFP
📅 June 22, 2026
🕐 9 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Frequently Asked Questions

Do veterans need Medicare if they have VA benefits?

Yes — most veterans should enroll in Medicare even with VA benefits. VA benefits only cover care at VA facilities or VA-approved providers. If you need emergency care outside a VA facility, Medicare provides essential backup coverage. Medicare also allows you to see non-VA specialists and use non-VA hospitals anywhere in the US.

Is VA coverage better than Medicare?

VA coverage and Medicare serve different purposes and are not directly comparable. VA care is often excellent and has lower out-of-pocket costs for veterans with service-connected disabilities. However, VA care is limited to VA facilities and approved providers. Medicare offers nationwide access to any Medicare-accepting provider. Most experts recommend having both.

Do veterans pay for Medicare Part B?

Yes — veterans must pay for Medicare Part B separately from their VA benefits. The standard Part B premium is $174.70 per month in 2026, regardless of VA status. Veterans with service-connected disabilities may qualify for additional VA benefits, but VA coverage does not waive the Medicare Part B premium.

Does the VA cover prescription drugs like Medicare Part D?

The VA provides comprehensive prescription drug coverage through the VA formulary at very low copays ($5–$11 per 30-day supply). Most veterans with VA drug coverage do not need Medicare Part D. However, if you ever lose VA eligibility or want access to non-VA pharmacies, having Part D protects you from late enrollment penalties.

Can veterans get free hearing aids through the VA?

Yes — veterans with service-connected hearing loss can receive hearing aids at no cost through the VA. Veterans with non-service-connected hearing loss may also qualify depending on disability rating and other eligibility criteria. This is a significantly better benefit than Medicare, which does not cover hearing aids under Original Medicare.

Approximately 9 million of the 65 million Medicare beneficiaries are veterans who also have access to VA healthcare. Navigating two separate federal healthcare systems is confusing — and making the wrong decisions about enrollment can cost veterans thousands of dollars or leave critical gaps in coverage. Here is exactly how the two systems work together.

The Critical Difference to Understand

VA healthcare and Medicare are entirely separate systems that do not coordinate with each other:

  • VA healthcare covers you at VA facilities and VA-approved providers — and nothing else (except in certain emergency situations)
  • Medicare covers you at any Medicare-accepting provider anywhere in the United States — which includes nearly all hospitals and physicians

When you receive care at a VA facility, Medicare does not pay anything — and when you receive care at a non-VA Medicare provider, the VA pays nothing. The systems are parallel, not integrated.

Should Veterans Enroll in Medicare Part B?

This is the most important decision veterans face at 65. The answer for most veterans is yes — and here is why:

Reasons to enroll in Part B despite VA coverage:

  • Emergency coverage outside VA: If you have a medical emergency far from a VA facility, Medicare covers you at any emergency room in the US. VA emergency coverage outside VA facilities is limited and complex.
  • Specialist access: Wait times at VA facilities for specialist care can be significant. Medicare allows you to see any specialist anywhere without referral restrictions.
  • Future protection: VA eligibility can change with budget decisions, priority group changes, or change of residence. Medicare is permanent once enrolled.
  • Non-VA hospital coverage: If your preferred local hospital is not a VA facility, Medicare is your coverage there.

The penalty for delaying Part B:

If you delay Medicare Part B enrollment past your Initial Enrollment Period without other qualifying coverage (employer insurance), you face a permanent 10% premium penalty per year of delay — even if VA coverage was your reason for not enrolling. VA coverage alone does not qualify as "creditable coverage" to avoid Part B late enrollment penalties.

⚠️ Common and Costly Mistake

Many veterans delay or skip Medicare Part B enrollment because they believe VA coverage is sufficient. When they later need non-VA care — due to emergency, relocation, or VA waitlist delays — they either have no coverage or face permanent premium penalties. Enroll in Part B at 65 unless you have employer-sponsored insurance from current employment.

Part D and VA Prescription Drug Coverage

VA prescription drug coverage is generally excellent — copays of $5–$11 per 30-day supply for most medications — and is considered "creditable" for Medicare Part D purposes. This means veterans with VA drug coverage can delay Part D enrollment without penalty as long as they have VA coverage.

However, consider enrolling in a low-cost Part D plan ($7–$15/month) as a backup, because:

  • VA drug coverage only applies at VA pharmacies and approved mail-order
  • If you need a prescription filled at a community pharmacy urgently, VA coverage may not apply
  • VA formulary does not include all medications — some newer drugs may not be available through VA

Key VA Benefits That Exceed Medicare

For veterans with service-connected disabilities, certain VA benefits far exceed what Medicare provides:

  • Hearing aids: Free for service-connected hearing loss — Medicare does not cover hearing aids at all
  • Dental care: Comprehensive dental for eligible veterans — Medicare covers almost no dental
  • Vision care: Routine vision exams and eyeglasses for eligible veterans — Medicare covers minimal vision
  • Prescription drugs: Very low copays vs. Medicare Part D cost-sharing
  • Mental health: Comprehensive mental health services at VA facilities with shorter wait times than many community providers
  • Long-term care: Community Living Centers (VA nursing homes) and home-based primary care for eligible veterans

CHAMPVA for Surviving Spouses and Dependents

CHAMPVA (Civilian Health and Medical Program of the Department of Veterans Affairs) provides healthcare coverage for spouses and dependents of veterans who died of service-connected causes or have a 100% permanent service-connected disability. CHAMPVA works alongside Medicare for eligible beneficiaries, covering Medicare cost-sharing — functioning similarly to a Medigap plan at no premium cost.

  • Enroll in Medicare Part B at 65 — do not assume VA coverage is sufficient substitute
  • Verify whether your VA coverage qualifies as creditable coverage for Part D to avoid penalties
  • Ask your VA social worker about all benefits you may be entitled to based on your disability rating
  • If you have hearing loss, contact the VA audiology department before buying hearing aids anywhere else
  • Surviving spouses — ask about CHAMPVA eligibility at va.gov/health-care/family-caregiver-benefits/champva
  • For complex veterans/Medicare questions, SHIP counselors can help for free — find yours at shiphelp.org
