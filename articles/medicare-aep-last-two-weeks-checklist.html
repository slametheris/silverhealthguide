Medicare Open Enrollment Ends December 7: The Last-Two-Weeks Checklist

SM
By Dr. Sandra Mills, RN
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Medicare's Annual Enrollment Period closes on December 7, and changes take effect January 1, 2027. If you do nothing, your plan simply renews with whatever changes it made. That can be fine — or it can cost you hundreds of dollars. Here is how to use the final stretch well.

⏰ Deadline

Submit any plan change by December 7. Don't wait for the last day — enrollment sites and phone lines get busy.

Step 1 — Gather Your Information (15 minutes)

  • Your Medicare card and current plan card
  • Every medication: name, strength, and how many per month
  • Your pharmacy and every doctor you see regularly
  • Your Annual Notice of Change (the letter your plan mailed in the fall)

Step 2 — Read What's Changing in Your Current Plan

Your Annual Notice of Change lists the new premium, deductible, copays, drug tiers, and network changes. Pay special attention to any drug you take that moved to a higher tier, gained prior authorization, or was removed. See our guide to reading the ANOC.

Step 3 — Compare Plans on Medicare.gov

  1. Go to Medicare.gov/plan-compare and enter your ZIP code
  2. Enter every medication and your pharmacy
  3. Sort by total estimated annual cost, not monthly premium
  4. Look at the top few plans and check each plan's star rating

Step 4 — Confirm the Details by Phone

Before switching, call your doctors' offices and ask whether they accept the plan for 2027. Ask your pharmacy whether it is in the plan's preferred network. Directories online can be out of date.

Step 5 — Check the 2027 Numbers

For 2027, the Part D out-of-pocket cap rises to $2,400 and the maximum Part D deductible rises to $700. The standard Part B premium rises to $209.40. See the full 2027 cost breakdown. A plan that was cheapest for you in 2026 may not be in 2027.

Step 6 — Enroll Early and Confirm

You can enroll through Plan Finder, by calling 1-800-MEDICARE, or through the plan. When you enroll in a new plan, your old plan ends automatically. Save your confirmation number, and check MyMedicare.gov a week later. Your new member card should arrive before January 1.

Need Help? It's Free

Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) offers free, unbiased counseling — find yours at shiphelp.org. Be cautious of unsolicited calls pushing you to switch; see how to spot enrollment scams.

  • Gather medications, doctors, and your Annual Notice of Change
  • Compare on Medicare.gov by total annual cost
  • Verify doctors and pharmacy by phone
  • Enroll before December 7 and save your confirmation
  • If you are happy with your plan, you don't need to do anything

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the last day of Medicare open enrollment?

The Annual Enrollment Period runs October 15 through December 7. Changes you make take effect January 1, 2027.

What happens if I do nothing during open enrollment?

Your current Medicare Advantage or Part D plan continues into 2027 with whatever changes it announced. That is fine if you reviewed those changes and are comfortable with them.

Can I change my mind after I enroll?

Yes. You can make additional changes through December 7, and your most recent enrollment is the one that takes effect on January 1.

Does open enrollment let me change my Medigap policy?

No. Medigap has its own rules. AEP applies to Medicare Advantage and Part D plans.
