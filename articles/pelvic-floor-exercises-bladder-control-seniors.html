Pelvic Floor Exercises for Bladder Control After 60: How to Do Them Right

JW
By Dr. James Whitfield, PT, DPT
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Bladder leaks affect many older adults, but they're often treatable. Strengthening the pelvic floor muscles — correctly — and adjusting a few bladder habits can reduce leaks for many people. Here is how to start.

Find the Right Muscles

The pelvic floor muscles are the ones you would tighten to stop passing gas or to stop the flow of urine. To locate them, you may briefly try to stop urine midstream once — but don't make this a regular exercise, because stopping urine repeatedly can interfere with normal emptying. Once you know the feeling, practice with an empty bladder.

How to Do a Kegel

  1. Sit or lie down with your muscles relaxed.
  2. Tighten the pelvic floor and lift gently upward and inward.
  3. Hold for 3 to 5 seconds while breathing normally.
  4. Relax fully for the same amount of time.
  5. Repeat about 10 times, three times a day.

Common mistakes: holding your breath, squeezing your buttocks or thighs, pushing down instead of lifting up, and skipping the relaxation phase. Many people notice improvement after several weeks of consistent practice, but results vary.

Bladder Habits That Help

  • Go on a regular schedule rather than waiting until the urge is strong
  • Limit caffeine, alcohol, and carbonated drinks if they trigger urgency
  • Stay hydrated — too little fluid can irritate the bladder
  • Treat constipation, which puts pressure on the bladder
  • Maintain a healthy weight; extra weight adds pressure

Pelvic Floor Physical Therapy

If you aren't sure you're doing Kegels correctly, or if home exercises aren't helping, a physical therapist trained in pelvic floor care can assess your muscles and teach you. Medicare Part B covers outpatient physical therapy when it's medically necessary and ordered by your doctor. This is often a better first step than surgery or medication for many types of leaks.

Managing Leaks While You Build Strength

Strengthening takes time. In the meantime, absorbent, washable underwear designed for light leaks can help you stay active without worrying.

See a Doctor If You Notice…

  • Blood in your urine, pain or burning, or fever
  • Sudden onset of leaks, or new leg weakness or numbness
  • Difficulty emptying your bladder or a weak stream
  • Leaks that significantly limit your daily life
  • Practice pelvic floor exercises daily — consistency matters most
  • Relax fully between squeezes and keep breathing
  • Ask for a pelvic floor physical therapy referral if you need guidance
  • Report warning signs to your doctor promptly

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Kegel exercises work for older adults?

Many people, including older adults, improve with consistent pelvic floor training. Technique matters, and a physical therapist can help you check it.

Does Medicare cover pelvic floor therapy?

Medicare Part B covers outpatient physical therapy when it is medically necessary and ordered by your doctor.

Are Kegels only for women?

No. Men can benefit too, including after prostate surgery.
