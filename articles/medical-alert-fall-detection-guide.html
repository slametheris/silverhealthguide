Medical Alert Systems With Fall Detection: What to Look For Before You Buy

JW
By Dr. James Whitfield, PT, DPT
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

For seniors who live alone, a medical alert system can mean the difference between lying on the floor for hours and getting help in minutes. But the products vary a lot. This guide explains the options, the limits of fall detection, and what to check before you sign up.

Types of Systems

  • At-home systems: a base unit that connects through a landline or cellular signal, plus a wearable button. Best if you spend most of your time at home.
  • Mobile (on-the-go) systems: a wearable with cellular service and GPS that works outside the home as well.
  • Smartwatch-style devices: may add fall detection and heart-rate features, but battery life and monitoring services vary.

How Fall Detection Works — and Its Limits

Fall detection uses sensors to notice a sudden drop and a lack of movement, then contacts the monitoring center automatically. It is a useful backup if you are unconscious or unable to press the button. But no system detects every fall, and some slow slides to the floor may not trigger it. Treat automatic detection as an addition to the help button, not a replacement. Press the button if you can.

What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Monitoring: Is it staffed 24/7 by trained operators, and how fast do they typically respond?
  2. Contract: Is there a long-term contract, and what are the cancellation terms?
  3. Battery and range: How long does the wearable last, and how far does it work from the base?
  4. Waterproofing: Falls often happen in the bathroom — can you wear it in the shower?
  5. GPS: Do you need protection away from home?
  6. Total cost: Compare monthly fees, equipment, and activation charges. Monthly fees commonly run from roughly $25 to $60 depending on features; check current pricing.
  7. Testing: Test the system monthly so you know it works.

What Does Medicare Cover?

Original Medicare does not generally cover medical alert systems. Some Medicare Advantage plans include a personal emergency response benefit, and some Medicaid programs and Area Agencies on Aging help with the cost. Ask your plan, and see Medicare Advantage extra benefits.

  • Choose a system that fits how you live: home only or on the go
  • Keep pressing the button as your first line — fall detection is a backup
  • Wear the device all the time, including in the bathroom if it's waterproof
  • Check whether your Medicare Advantage plan includes a benefit
  • Test the system once a month

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Medicare pay for medical alert systems?

Original Medicare generally does not. Some Medicare Advantage plans, Medicaid waiver programs, and local agencies offer assistance.

Does fall detection catch every fall?

No. It can miss some falls, particularly slow ones. Use the help button whenever you can.

Do I need a landline?

Not necessarily. Many systems use cellular service. Ask what works in your area.
