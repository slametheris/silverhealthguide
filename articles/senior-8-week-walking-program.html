Frequently Asked Questions

How far should a 70-year-old walk per day?

Most seniors should target 30 minutes of moderate walking on most days — approximately 3,000–5,000 steps. Three 10-minute walks provide the same benefit as one 30-minute walk.

Is walking 30 minutes daily enough exercise?

For sedentary seniors, consistent 30-minute daily walking reduces cardiovascular disease risk, improves blood sugar, reduces fall risk, and supports brain health. Complement with strength training 2 days/week.

How do I start walking after years of inactivity?

Start with 5–10 minutes of easy walking and increase by 2–3 minutes each week. Consistency over intensity — 10 minutes daily every day beats 45 minutes once per week.

Can seniors with arthritis walk for exercise?

Yes — walking lubricates joints and strengthens supporting muscles. Start slower and shorter, use soft surfaces, and warm up with 5 minutes of slow walking first.

Walking 30 minutes daily reduces cardiovascular disease risk by 35%, improves blood sugar, strengthens fall-prevention muscles, and supports brain health. This 8-week program takes you there from wherever you are now.

The Talk Test — Your Pace Guide

Walk fast enough that full sentences take slight effort — but you are not gasping. If you can recite a poem comfortably, walk faster. If you can't say more than 2 words at a time, slow down. This moderate intensity produces the most cardiovascular benefit for seniors.

Modifications

Arthritis: Walk on softer surfaces (grass, track, treadmill). Ice joints 10 minutes after if inflamed. Balance problems: Walk near walls, use poles, choose flat terrain. Heart conditions: Follow your cardiologist's target heart rate and stay within it.
