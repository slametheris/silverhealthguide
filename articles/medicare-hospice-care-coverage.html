Medicare Hospice Coverage: What It Covers, Who Qualifies, and What Families Need to Know

RO
By Dr. Rachel Okonkwo, MD
📅 July 27, 2026
🕐 10 min read

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Medicare cover hospice care?

Yes — Medicare Part A covers hospice care comprehensively for terminally ill beneficiaries with a life expectancy of 6 months or less if the illness runs its natural course. Hospice coverage includes physician and nursing care, medications related to the terminal diagnosis, medical equipment, aide services, social work, counseling, spiritual care, and bereavement support for the family — all at $0 copay.

How do you qualify for Medicare hospice?

To qualify for Medicare hospice, a patient must be enrolled in Medicare Part A, have a terminal illness with a prognosis of 6 months or less if the disease runs its normal course (certified by two physicians), and choose to receive comfort-focused care rather than curative treatment. Importantly, choosing hospice does not mean giving up — it means shifting the goal from curing to maximizing quality of life and comfort.

Is hospice care free with Medicare?

Yes — Medicare covers hospice care at $0 copay for all covered services. There is no deductible and no coinsurance for hospice care. The only potential costs are a small copay for outpatient prescription drugs ($5 maximum per drug) and 5% of the Medicare-approved amount for inpatient respite care.

How long does Medicare cover hospice?

Medicare hospice benefits are provided in benefit periods: two initial 90-day periods followed by unlimited 60-day periods as long as the patient continues to meet eligibility criteria. There is no overall time limit — if the patient remains terminally ill, hospice benefits continue indefinitely. A patient can also leave hospice and return to regular Medicare if they want to pursue curative treatment.

What is the most important thing families should know about hospice?

The most important thing: most families access hospice too late. Studies show that families who enroll in hospice earlier report significantly higher satisfaction, better quality of life for the patient, and sometimes even longer survival times. What Medicare Hospice Covers — Comprehensive and Free

The Medicare hospice benefit is one of the most comprehensive in the entire Medicare program. At $0 copay, it covers:

  • Physician services from the hospice medical director and attending physician
  • Skilled nursing care — visits as frequently as medically necessary, 24/7 phone access
  • Medications for pain relief, symptom control, and comfort related to the terminal diagnosis
  • Medical equipment — hospital bed, wheelchair, walker, oxygen, bedside commode, all delivered to the home
  • Home health aide services — bathing, personal care, grooming
  • Physical, occupational, and speech therapy for comfort and function
  • Social worker services — care coordination, community resources, family support
  • Spiritual care / chaplaincy for patients and families of all faiths
  • Bereavement counseling for the family for up to 13 months after the patient's death
  • Respite care — short-term inpatient care to give caregivers a break (5% copay)
  • Continuous home care — intensive nursing during crisis periods

How to Access Hospice — The Process

The process begins with your doctor. If you or a family member has a serious, life-limiting illness, ask directly: "Would this patient benefit from a hospice evaluation?" Two physicians must certify that the patient has a prognosis of 6 months or less if the disease runs its normal course.

Choosing hospice does not mean giving up hope or stopping all treatment. It means shifting the primary goal from curing the disease to maximizing comfort, dignity, and quality of life. Patients can withdraw from hospice at any time if they change their mind and want to pursue curative treatment — and can then re-elect hospice if needed.

Where Hospice Care Is Provided

Most hospice care — approximately 70% — is provided at home. Hospice nurses, aides, and social workers visit regularly, and the hospice team is available by phone 24 hours a day. Hospice care is also provided in nursing facilities, assisted living communities, hospice residential facilities, and hospitals. The location is chosen based on patient and family preference and clinical needs.

The Most Harmful Myth About Hospice

The most damaging hospice myth: that choosing hospice means the patient will die sooner. Multiple studies show the opposite. A landmark 2010 NEJM study of lung cancer patients found those who chose hospice lived 2.7 months longer than those who received aggressive treatment — with significantly better quality of life and less distress. The intensive symptom management, reduced hospitalization, and reduction in treatment side effects that hospice provides appears to extend life in some patients while dramatically improving its quality.

How to Start the Conversation

Many families avoid discussing hospice because it feels like giving up. A reframe that helps: "I want to make sure you have the most support possible and the best quality of life for whatever time we have." Ask your doctor: "What would hospice look like for our situation? What would we gain from it?" Most hospice organizations also offer free consultations — calling a local hospice for information is not a commitment to enroll.

  • Ask your doctor about hospice eligibility if your loved one has a serious life-limiting illness — do not wait until the last days
  • Understand that hospice is fully covered by Medicare Part A — $0 copay for all covered services
  • Know that patients can leave hospice and return to regular Medicare treatment at any time
  • Contact your local hospice organization for a free consultation — it is not a commitment
  • Bereavement support continues for the family for 13 months after a loved one's death — access it
