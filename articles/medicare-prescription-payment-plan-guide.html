The Medicare Prescription Payment Plan: Spread Your Drug Costs Across the Year

MC
By Margaret Chen, PharmD
🕐 6 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

The Medicare Prescription Payment Plan (M3P) doesn't lower your drug costs — it changes when you pay them. Instead of paying a large amount at the pharmacy counter, you pay your plan a bill each month. For people with high drug costs early in the year, that can make budgeting much easier.

How It Works

  1. You opt in with your Part D plan (a phone call or online form).
  2. At the pharmacy, you pay $0 for covered drugs.
  3. Your plan pays the pharmacy and then sends you a monthly bill for what you owe.
  4. The plan spreads your costs across the remaining months of the year, so you pay roughly equal installments.

There is no interest and no fee for participating. Your total out-of-pocket drug spending for the year is still limited by the Part D cap — $2,400 in 2027.

Who Benefits Most

  • People who take one or more expensive drugs and would otherwise pay hundreds of dollars in a single month
  • People who hit their deductible early in the year
  • People who prefer predictable monthly payments

It is usually less useful if your drug costs are already small, or if you receive Extra Help, because your copays are already low.

What to Watch Out For

  • You still owe the same total. M3P spreads costs; it doesn't reduce them.
  • Bills must be paid. Missing payments can lead to losing M3P participation and, in some cases, further consequences with your plan. Ask your plan how it handles non-payment.
  • Leaving your plan. If you change plans mid-year, any unpaid balance generally still has to be paid.
  • Renewal. Plans may renew your participation automatically from year to year. Confirm your status with your plan each fall.

How to Join

Call the member services number on your Part D or Medicare Advantage drug plan card, or use the plan's website or member portal. Ask: "I'd like to opt in to the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan for 2027." Your pharmacy can also tell you if you might benefit when a prescription costs a lot.

  • Ask your plan whether M3P suits your expected 2027 drug costs
  • Opt in before your first expensive fill of the year if possible
  • Budget for the monthly bill — it's not optional once you join
  • Confirm your enrollment status each fall

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan free?

Yes. There is no fee and no interest for participating. You still owe the same total drug costs, just in monthly installments.

Does it lower my drug costs?

No. It spreads your out-of-pocket costs across the year. The Part D cap ($2,400 in 2027) still limits your total.

Do I have to join every year?

Plans may renew your participation automatically, but you should confirm your status with your plan each fall.

Is it a good idea if I have Extra Help?

Usually not necessary, because Extra Help already keeps your copays low.
