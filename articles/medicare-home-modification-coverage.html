Does Medicare Cover Home Modifications? Grab Bars, Ramps, and Aging-in-Place Equipment

SM
By Dr. Sandra Mills, RN
🕐 7 min read
✅ Expert reviewed

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Medicare cover grab bars and bathroom modifications?

Original Medicare does not cover grab bar installation or most home modifications like ramp construction or bathroom remodeling. However, some Medicare Advantage plans include a home modification or home safety benefit worth $500–$1,500 annually that can cover grab bars, ramps, stair lifts, and similar modifications. Call your Medicare Advantage plan and ask specifically: 'What home modification or home safety benefits do I have?'

Does Medicare cover stair lifts or wheelchair ramps?

Original Medicare does not cover stair lifts or wheelchair ramp construction — these are home improvements, not durable medical equipment in Medicare's classification. Some Medicare Advantage plans include these under supplemental home modification benefits. State Medicaid waiver programs often cover home modifications for low-income dual-eligible seniors to prevent nursing home placement.

What home equipment does Medicare cover?

Medicare Part B covers durable medical equipment (DME) when prescribed by a physician for medical necessity. Covered equipment includes: hospital beds, wheelchairs and power scooters, walkers and rollators, crutches, CPAP/BiPAP machines, blood glucose monitors, oxygen equipment, and bedside commodes. This equipment is covered at 80% after the Part B deductible from Medicare-enrolled suppliers.

Are there free programs for senior home modifications?

Yes — several programs provide free or low-cost home modifications: Rebuilding Together (rebuildingtogether.org) provides free home repairs and modifications for low-income seniors. Area Agencies on Aging often administer home modification programs funded by the Older Americans Act. Some Community Development Block Grant programs fund modifications through local governments. HUD's Section 504 Home Repair program provides grants up to $10,000 for very low-income seniors 62+.

Can I deduct home modifications for medical reasons on my taxes?

Medical home modifications may be tax-deductible as medical expenses if they are medically necessary and don't increase your home's value. Qualifying examples: ramps, grab bars, widened doorways for wheelchair access, elevator installation for mobility limitations. Cosmetic improvements (replacing fixtures with more attractive accessible versions) are not deductible. Consult a tax professional — medical deductions require itemizing and exceed a threshold based on your AGI.

Aging in place is the overwhelming preference of American seniors — and the right home modifications can make it safe and sustainable for years longer than without them. Understanding exactly what Medicare covers, what Medicare Advantage may add, and what free programs exist helps seniors make their home safer without unnecessary out-of-pocket expense.

What Original Medicare Covers — and Doesn't

Original Medicare Part B covers durable medical equipment (DME) — not home improvements. The distinction matters:

  • Medicare DOES cover (with physician order): Bedside commodes, hospital beds for home, rollator walkers, wheelchairs and power scooters, bath benches and shower chairs when prescribed, handheld shower attachments prescribed as DME, stair-climbing wheelchairs in limited circumstances
  • Medicare does NOT cover: Grab bar installation, wheelchair ramp construction, stair lifts, walk-in tub conversion, bathroom remodeling, doorway widening, smart home technology

Medicare Advantage Home Modification Benefits

This is where significant variation exists. Many Medicare Advantage plans include supplemental benefits not in Original Medicare — and home modification or home safety benefits are increasingly common. These plans may cover:

  • Grab bar installation ($150–$500 value)
  • Non-slip bath strips and safety mats
  • Handrails for indoor stairs
  • Door handle replacements (lever vs. round knob)
  • Ramp installation for entryways

Coverage amounts vary significantly — $200 to $1,500 annually depending on the plan. To find out: call your plan's member services and ask specifically: "What home modification or home safety benefit do I have and how do I access it?" Many seniors have this benefit and never use it.

During AEP (October 15–December 7), compare Medicare Advantage plans specifically on home modification benefits if you anticipate needing these services next year. Medicare.gov Plan Finder lists supplemental benefits by plan.

Free and Low-Cost Programs

Rebuilding Together

A national nonprofit that provides free home repairs and modifications for low-income seniors, people with disabilities, and veterans. Services include grab bar installation, ramp construction, weatherization, and general safety repairs. Find your local affiliate at rebuildingtogether.org.

Area Agencies on Aging

Federally funded through the Older Americans Act, AAAs in most counties administer home modification programs, minor home repair assistance, and weatherization programs for seniors. Find your local AAA at eldercare.acl.gov.

HUD Section 504 Home Repair Program

Provides grants up to $10,000 for home repairs (not repaid) and loans up to $40,000 for very low-income rural homeowners 62+. Covers accessibility modifications including ramps and grab bars. Apply through your local USDA Rural Development office.

State Medicaid Waiver Programs

For dual-eligible seniors (Medicare + Medicaid), many states have Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waiver programs that cover home modifications to prevent nursing home placement. Coverage varies significantly by state. Contact your state Medicaid office or local AAA for program availability.
