Frequently Asked Questions

How should seniors organize multiple medications?

Use a 7-day AM/PM pill organizer filled weekly on the same day. Keep a printed medication list in your wallet and on the refrigerator. Store at room temperature away from humidity. Review all medications with your pharmacist annually.

What is polypharmacy and why is it dangerous?

Taking five or more medications simultaneously — affecting 40%+ of seniors. Each additional drug increases drug-drug interaction risk. Annual medication reviews can eliminate unnecessary prescriptions and reduce side effects.

How do I dispose of unused medications?

Use a DEA-authorized medication take-back site (many pharmacies). If unavailable, mix with coffee grounds in a sealed bag and discard in trash. Never flush medications.

Medication errors injure 1.5 million Americans annually. These three systems prevent most errors.

Three-Step System

Step 1 — Weekly Pill Organizer

Fill a 7-day AM/PM organizer on the same day each week in good light with your medication bottles in front of you. A full compartment tells you instantly if you missed a dose.

Step 2 — Master Medication List

Printed list of every medication — name, dose, frequency, purpose, prescriber. Keep copies in your wallet, on the refrigerator (for emergency responders), and in your medical file. Update every time a medication changes.

Step 3 — Annual Polypharmacy Review

If you take five or more medications, schedule an annual review with your pharmacist. Bring every pill bottle including vitamins and OTC drugs. Ask: Does each medication still serve a purpose? Are there interactions? Are any on the Beers Criteria inappropriate for older adults?

Safe Storage Rules

  • Avoid bathrooms — steam and humidity degrade tablets
  • Room temperature (59–77°F) for most medications
  • Refrigerate only what's labeled "refrigerate" — insulin, some liquids
  • Lock away if dementia patients or children in the home
  • Travel — carry-on only, bring 2 extra days' supply
